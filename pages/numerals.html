A Revised Neo-Sindarin Numeral Paradigm

Ascending Additive Order, Denomination-First Multiplication and Positional Grouping

Proposal - 24 October 2026
Status: Proposed Neo-Sindarin reconstruction, not an attested Tolkienian numeral grammar.
Scope: Decimal and duodecimal cardinal numerals. Ordinals, fractions and the syntax of numerals modifying external nouns require separate treatment.

1. Abstract

This proposal reconstructs a productive system of cardinal numerals for later Sindarin, drawing primarily on Tolkien's late Common Eldarin grammatical descriptions, earlier Qenya numeral paradigms, and the documented conventions of Sindarin mutation.

The proposed system follows four central principles:

  1. Ascending additive order: smaller numerical constituents precede larger ones.
  2. Denomination-first multiplication: a numerical denomination precedes the numeral that quantifies it.
  3. Hierarchical grouping: compound multipliers are grammatical constituents with explicitly defined scope.
  4. Morphological differentiation: denominations take the appropriate singular or plural form, while postposed modifiers may undergo soft mutation.

Conjunctions are permitted between additive constituents. This standard recommends their regular use at defined boundaries to maintain clarity and establish a canonical literary form.

The system resembles Malagasy in its ascending additive order, but differs from strictly positional Malagasy constructions by permitting compound multipliers of higher denominations.

2. Historical and Linguistic Foundations

2.1. Earlier Qenya: additive inversion and numeral-first multiplication

Tolkien's Early Qenya Grammar, published in Parma Eldalamberon 14, provides the following forms:

Early Qenya Value Construction
min yukainen 21 One + twenty
kea tuksa 110 Ten + hundred
yukainen tuksa 120 Twenty + hundred
yúyo tuksa 200 Two × hundred
nelde tukse 300 Three × hundreds
yúyo húmi 2,000 Two × thousands

Source: PE14, pp. 49–50, 82–83.

These examples provide evidence for ascending additive order extending beyond the tens, as well as multiplier-before-denomination constructions.

However, the Early Qenya Grammar explicitly places numerical modifiers before the nouns they qualify. Therefore, its multiplicative word order agrees with the general syntax of the language at that conceptual stage.

It does not necessarily establish the order Tolkien intended for later Sindarin.

2.2. Later Common Eldarin: numerals following nouns

In PE23, p. 142, Tolkien describes numeral adjectives other than 'one' as regularly following the nouns they qualify in Common Eldarin. He further states that this arrangement remained usual, though not obligatory, in the later languages.

This is supported by Tolkien's later Quenya examples, including:

  • elen atta — two stars
  • eleni nelde — three stars

Sources: PE23/142; VT49/45.

The significance is historical. Tolkien's later conception of numeral–noun syntax differs explicitly from that found in Early Qenya.

If higher numerical denominations behaved grammatically as quantified nouns, the expected later construction would similarly place the denomination first.

Thus the Early Qenya *nelde tukse* could correspond structurally to a later construction meaning 'hundreds three'.

This is a reasoned reconstruction, not a directly attested later numeral.

2.3. Numerical denominations as nouns

The Early Qenya distinction between yúyo tuksa and nelde tukse suggests that denominational words could participate in nominal number agreement.

Their inflection is therefore evidence for treating them as quantified nominal elements, rather than purely abstract mathematical operators.

For Neo-Sindarin, the present proposal adopts this analysis:

**[DENOMINATION] [NUMERAL MODIFIER]**

This allows the ordinary rules for noun number and postposed modifiers to inform the morphology of numerals.

2.4. Ascending addition

Tolkien's Early Qenya examples support smaller-before-larger additive constructions.

In addition, the English dialogue and narration of The Lord of the Rings repeatedly employ inverted tens, such as 'eight and thirty', 'six and twentieth' and 'two and forty'.

These passages are compatible with the proposed structure, but cannot establish Sindarin syntax directly, since the dialogue is represented through Tolkien's literary translation conventions.

Ascending addition is consequently adopted as a productive Neo-Sindarin rule, rather than presented as an independently attested late Sindarin rule.

2.5. Written numerals

The Tengwar numeral notation described in PE23, pp. 35–36, places the least significant numerical digit first.

However, Tolkien's accompanying English readings do not necessarily reproduce this order.

Written positional reversal therefore supplies compatible contextual evidence, but does not independently prove the ordering of spoken numerical expressions.

3. Proposed Grammatical Principles

Rule I - Ascending addition

When independent numerical values are added, their constituents occur in ascending order.

For example:

Value Literal structure
36 six thirty
136 six thirty hundred
236 six thirty hundred two
1,036 six thirty thousand

The English structures illustrate numerical relationships, not literal Sindarin morphology.

Rule II - Denomination-first multiplication

A higher numerical denomination precedes the multiplier that qualifies it.

Value Literal structure
200 hundreds two
400 hundreds four
2,000 thousands two
36,000 thousands six thirty
100,000 thousands hundred

A complete compound numeral may function as the multiplier of a denomination.

Consequently, the expression 'thousands six thirty' has the value:

1000 × (6 + 30) = 36,000

It does not mean *(1000 × 6) + 30*.

The boundaries of such compound multipliers are established by the grouping rules below.

Rule III - Canonical positional groups

For a number expressed in base (b), define the principal grouping denomination:

R = b3

The entire number is divided into groups of three digits:

N = g0 + g1R + g2R2 + …

where *0 ≤ gi < R*.

For decimal numeration, *R = 1,000*. For duodecimal numeration, *R = 1,00012 = 1,72810*.

The groups are expressed in ascending order, and groups with a value of zero are omitted.

A non-zero group is formed as follows:

  • The lowest group is expressed as an ordinary numeral below (R).
  • Each higher group begins with its denomination, followed by the group's coefficient.
  • A coefficient of one is normally unexpressed.
  • A coefficient greater than one follows its denomination as a complete numeral constituent.
  • The numerical value of a coefficient must be between 1 and (R-1).
  • Higher-scale denominations appear at most once each, and in ascending scale order.

Thus 36,036 is divided into two groups:

36 + (36 × 1000)

Its structure is:

six thirty AND thousands six thirty

Whereas 30,006 is:

6 + (30 × 1000)
six AND thousands thirty

And 6,030 is:

30 + (6 × 1000)
thirty AND thousands six

This grouping convention determines the scope of multiplication and prevents the same unmarked numerical sequence from receiving multiple canonical readings.

Rule IV - Internal structure of a three-digit group

Within each positional group, the components are ordered:

Units → decades → hundreds

The lowest two digits form a numeral constituent. Where a dedicated lexical form exists, it is used as one word.

A hundreds component is formed with the denomination first, followed by its multiplier.

For example:

Value Structure
36 six thirty
100 hundred
120 twenty AND hundred
200 hundreds two
236 six thirty AND hundred two
456 six fifty AND hundreds four

Multiplication within a three-digit group is restricted to the hundred denomination and a single-digit multiplier. Thus 3,600 is canonically decomposed as six hundreds plus three thousands, not as thirty-six hundreds.

An expression for a higher denomination may, however, take a complete coefficient of up to three digits.

Rule V - Conjunctions

The proposed canonical literary register uses Sindarin ***a(h)*** between independent additive components:

  • Between a lower component and a separate hundreds component.
  • Between independently expressed groups of thousands, millions and higher denominations.

No conjunction is inserted between the unit and decade elements of an ordinary two-digit numeral.

No conjunction is inserted between a denomination and its multiplier.

The conjunction expresses addition and does not modify the multiplicative scope of a denomination.

An abbreviated colloquial register may omit additive conjunctions where the group boundaries remain recoverable. Such omissions are permitted variations, not the canonical form specified here.

Rule VI - Grammatical number

The denomination behaves as a counted noun.

The recommended Neo-Sindarin agreement convention is:

  • Coefficient one: singular denomination.
  • Coefficients two and above: plural denomination.

The numeral modifier itself remains an uninflected cardinal form except for phonological mutation.

This adopts regular plural agreement for Sindarin, including after two.

The proposal recognises that later Quenya displays exceptional singular behaviour with atta 'two'. Whether a comparable exception existed in late Sindarin is unresolved, and the present rule must therefore be considered provisional.

Rule VII - Mutation

Two distinct mutation environments must be recognised.

A. Modifier mutation

In a denomination–multiplier construction, the numeral modifier follows the nominal denomination and may undergo §s{soft mutation}.

Underlying elements Proposed result
tych + tâd tych §s{dâd|tâd}
tych + canad tych §s{ganad|canad}
menig + tâd menig §s{dâd|tâd}
hyst + pae hyst §s{bae|pae}

The scope of mutation is the beginning of the numerical modifier, rather than every word within that modifier.

B. Conjunction mutation

The conjunction ***a(h)*** is treated independently. The canonical register provisionally adopts the §i{sibilant mutation} variant associated with ***a(h)***.

Thus:

  • a + toch → a §i{thoch|toch}
  • a + tych → a §i{thych|tych}
  • a + host → a §i{chost|host}
  • a + hyst → a §i{chyst|hyst}
  • a + hû → a §i{chû|hû}

The variant ***ah*** is used before vowels.

This §i{mutation} is not the same operation as the §s{soft mutation} of a numeral modifying a denomination.

Tolkien's late account in PE23/143 acknowledges variation in adjective mutation. Regularising these environments is therefore a Neo-Sindarin editorial choice.

5. Ambiguity Tests

The following examples were selected because earlier discussions identified potential difficulties distinguishing multiplication, addition and grammatical grouping.

Number Proposed Sindarin Arithmetic
1,002 tâd a meneg 2 + 1,000
2,000 menig §s{dâd|tâd} 1,000 × 2
1,036 eneg nelphaen a meneg 36 + 1,000
6,030 nelphaen a menig eneg 30 + 6,000
23,000 menig neledh taphaen 1,000 × (3 + 20)
30,006 eneg a menig nelphaen 6 + (1,000 × 30)
36,000 menig eneg nelphaen 1,000 × (6 + 30)
36,036 eneg nelphaen a menig eneg nelphaen 36 + 1,000 × 36
236,036 eneg nelphaen a menig eneg nelphaen a §i{thych|tych} §s{dâd|tâd} 36 + 1,000 × (6 + 30 + 200)

In the final example, the conjunction following nelphaen inside the multiplier belongs to the three-digit coefficient 236. The preceding menig governs that complete constituent.

A reader can recover the structure by first identifying the higher-scale denomination and then parsing its bounded three-digit coefficient.

Higher scales

For 2,036,036, the structure is:

six thirty AND thousands six thirty AND millions two

Or formally:

36 + (1000 × 36) + (1,000,000 × 2)

No Sindarin equivalent of million is supplied here, since a suitable lexical form has not been established for this proposal.

The grammatical method nevertheless extends to higher denominations once their vocabulary is defined.

6. Applying the Paradigm to Duodecimal Numerals

The same structural grammar can be applied to a base-twelve vocabulary.

The principal denominations are:

Duodecimal value Numerical meaning Proposed word
1012 Dozen rast
10012 Gross host
1,00012 Great gross (grossard) hû

The adopted plural forms for the higher denominations are ***hyst*** and ***hui***.

The lower numeral vocabulary, including dedicated forms such as ***tadrast***, ***paerast*** and ***rastodo***, follows the existing duodecimal lexical inventory.

The distinction between decimal ten (***pae***) and duodecimal dozen (***rast***) must be maintained throughout.

Detailed analysis of 36A1712

The number can be grouped as:

36 | A17

Its positional value is: 

  36A1712 = 7 + (1 × 12)
             + (10 × 144)
             + ((6 + 3 × 12) × 1,728)

Thus the expression consists of three additive components:

  1. ***rastodo*** - 1712 - literally dozen-seven.
  2. ***hyst §s{bae|pae}*** - ten grosses, with the plural denomination followed by the lenited multiplier ***§s{bae|pae}***, from ***pae***.
  3. ***hui eneg neledhrast*** - great grosses multiplied by six-three-dozen (3612).

The complete expression is therefore:

Rastodo a §i{chyst|hyst} §s{bae|pae} a §i{chui|hui} eneg neledhrast.

The conjunctions mark the independent components, and the denominator-first construction gives the final phrase its multiplication.

A crucial distinction is that *pae* means ten, whereas *rast* means a dozen. Accordingly, ten grosses must not be confused with twelve grosses.

The complete expression represents 74,035 in decimal, exactly the same quantity as the decimal example above.

7. Formal Production Rules

The grammatical rules may be expressed independently of Sindarin morphology. 

NUMBER :=
    GROUP (AND GROUP)*

GROUP :=
    SMALL_GROUP
    | SCALE
    | SCALE COEFFICIENT

SMALL_GROUP :=
    BELOW_HUNDRED
    | HUNDREDS_COMPONENT
    | BELOW_HUNDRED AND HUNDREDS_COMPONENT

BELOW_HUNDRED :=
    LEXICAL_NUMBER
    | DECADE
    | UNIT DECADE

HUNDREDS_COMPONENT :=
    HUNDRED
    | HUNDREDS_PLURAL DIGIT

COEFFICIENT :=
    SMALL_GROUP

SCALE :=
    THOUSAND
    | MILLION
    | BILLION
    | ...

SCALE COEFFICIENT :=
    SCALE_PLURAL COEFFICIENT

The following semantic constraints apply:

  1. Each SMALL_GROUP has a value from **1** through **b3-1**.
  2. The multiplier of a hundred has a value from **2** through **b-1**; a coefficient of one is implicit.
  3. A higher-scale coefficient has a value from **2** through **b3-1**; a coefficient of one is implicit.
  4. SCALE elements occur only in strictly ascending order.
  5. A scale always takes the following bounded coefficient as its multiplier.
  6. No coefficient may contain another scale denomination.
  7. Additive conjunctions may occur within a coefficient but do not terminate the scope of its governing scale.
  8. Zero-valued positional components are omitted.
  9. The canonical register uses the established lexical numeral for a value wherever the adopted vocabulary provides one.

These restrictions provide a deterministic production procedure for non-negative integers within the available numerical vocabulary.

The grammar defines a single canonical structural expression per value, assuming that lexical variants and optional colloquial conjunction deletion are excluded from the canonical register.

8. Important Limitations

This proposal depends on several reconstructions that must remain explicitly identified.

**Denominational syntax**. Tolkien's later noun–numeral order is attested, but its application to large Sindarin numeral denominations is inferred.

**Additive inversion**. Early Qenya supports ascending addition, but its complete productivity in later Sindarin has not been demonstrated directly.

**Compound multipliers**. No identified late Sindarin attestation establishes a complex numeral functioning as the multiplier of a thousand or higher denomination. The use of such coefficients is a productive extension.

**Agreement after two**. Singular agreement with atta occurs in late Quenya. The adoption of plural Sindarin denominations following two remains provisional.

**Mutation**. Regular soft mutation of postposed numeral modifiers is a reasonable Neo-Sindarin convention. Tolkien's late writings also acknowledge variation in adjective mutation. Likewise, conjunction-induced mutation requires an explicit choice among competing historical variants.

**Lexical status**. Meneg is supported through Menegroth, although its precise numerical interpretation has been questioned. Host is an earlier Noldorin form for gross, and its use in later Neo-Sindarin is an adaptation. The decimal toch and duodecimal hû, as employed here, belong to the provisional lexical standard rather than a demonstrated complete late Sindarin counting paradigm.

**Number base**. Decimal and duodecimal systems coexist in Tolkien's conception. Their spoken forms must be interpreted according to the selected numerical base; superficially similar expressions cannot automatically be treated as interchangeable.

9. Conclusion

The proposed Neo-Sindarin paradigm adopts the following structure:

Ascending addition + denomination-first multiplication + bounded positional coefficients + nominal agreement and mutation.

Its strongest historical motivation is the change between Early Qenya numeral–noun syntax and Tolkien's later Common Eldarin description of postposed numeral modifiers.

Its strongest practical feature is that it can distinguish potentially confusing numerical expressions without requiring the listener to infer arithmetic operations from unrestricted word sequences.

Conjunctions and formal grouping conventions are retained to make the system easier to analyse, teach and use consistently.

The paradigm is therefore recommended as a coherent Neo-Sindarin reconstruction informed by Tolkien's later grammar, rather than a claim to have recovered the exact historical counting system of the Sindar.
