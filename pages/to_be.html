Reconstruction of the "to be" verb

This is extremely speculative and not at all authoritative. If you want a conservative and source-based paradigm, walk away now! This is akin to my personal notes.

Based on Salo's analysis of the "to be" verb, and based on the fact that a lot of information has been disclosed since the publication of his book, I've decided to attempt a reconstruction myself.

David Salo treated *na-* as a derived **A-stem verb**. Other Neo-Sindarin analysts treat it instead as a **monosyllabic basic verb**, derived directly from the primitive root **√NA** ("be, exist"). The elephant in the room is that no attested basic verbs end in vowels. So a large portion of the content below include some speculative assumptions.

Present tense

This reconstruction treats the copular verb *na-* as an irregular monosyllabic verb descended from Primitive Eldarin √NĀ, rather than as an ordinary derived A-stem. The goal is an internally consistent and phonologically plausible Neo-Sindarin conjugation, drawing on Tolkien's attested pronominal elements, the Sindarin verbal suffixes, and documented historical sound changes.

**None of the complete Sindarin copular forms proposed below is directly attested in Tolkien's writings.** The reconstructions therefore remain hypothetical, even where their individual components and phonological developments are supported.

Historical and phonological foundations

The primitive root √NĀ, "be (the same as another), exist", occurs in Tolkien's linguistic writings, including *PE17:93*, *PE22:147* and *VT49:27–31*. Sources:

The developmental sequences below are proposed phonological models. Some intermediate steps and their precise historical ordering remain uncertain, particularly because Tolkien revised the phonological history of Sindarin repeatedly.

Third-person singular: *naw* — "he/she/it is"

Primitive Eldarin *nā-* is taken as the verbal stem. As with many Sindarin verbs, the uninflected present serves as the third-person singular. The inherited long vowel follows its normal historical development: 

    ✶nā → OS nǭ → S nau → naw

The final spelling *-aw* represents the diphthong *au* in word-final position. This approach does not require extending the late basic-verb vowel-lengthening rule (*car-* → *câr*) to an otherwise unattested class of monosyllabic vowel-final basic verbs.

**Status:** Plausibly derived Neo-Sindarin reconstruction. The assumption that primitive *nā* itself served as the unsuffixed present form remains hypothetical.

First-person singular: *naun* — "I am"

The first-person singular pronoun ✶*ni*, "I, me", is attested in *VT49:50, 52* and elsewhere. Assuming the pronoun became a verbal suffix in Primitive Eldarin: 

    ✶nā + ✶ni → ✶nāni
    ✶nāni → nāne → nān → nǭn → naun

The changes are:

  1. Final short *-i* becomes *-e*.
  2. Final short *-e* disappears.
  3. Long *ā* develops into *ǭ*.
  4. *ǭ* develops into *au*.

This also explains why the short Sindarin first-person suffix *-n* produces the same result: the full primitive pronoun *-ni* has lost its final vowel. The proposed form therefore converges with the simpler formation: 

    ✶nā-n → ✶nān → naun

**Status:** Plausibly derived Neo-Sindarin reconstruction. The pronoun *ni* is attested, but the primitive conjugation *nāni* is hypothetical.

Source: Eldamo: ✶ni, *VT49:50, 52*.

First-person plural exclusive: *nauf* — "we are, excluding you"

The Primitive Elvish pronoun ✶*me*, "we (exclusive)", is attested in *VT49:50, 52*. Tolkien's 1969 Sindarin verbal material contains the exclusive suffix *-f* /v/, as in *athof* (*PE22:167*), and Paul Strack suggests that this ending represents a historical development of earlier *-m(e)*.

However, combining *nā-* and *-me* in Primitive Eldarin creates a phonological difficulty: 

    ✶nāme → nām → nǭm → naum → nauv → naw

The final *v* disappears after *au*, following the development discussed by Tolkien in *WJ:414*. To preserve a productive first-person exclusive distinction, the proposed paradigm therefore adopts **late morphological suffixation**. The copular stem and the exclusive ending develop independently, and the completed form is then constructed: 

    ✶nā  → S naw
    ✶-me ⇝ S -f /v/
  
    naw + -f → nauf

The spelling *au* replaces final *aw* before the added consonant.

**Status:** Hypothetical late Neo-Sindarin morphological formation, supported by an attested Sindarin pronominal ending. It is not the regular uninterrupted phonological outcome of primitive *nāme*.

Sources:

  • Eldamo: ✶me
  • Eldamo: Subject suffixes, *PE22:167*
  • Loss of final *v* after *au*, *WJ:414*

First-person plural inclusive: *naub* — "we are, including you"

The inclusive first-person plural ending *-b* occurs in Tolkien's 1969 Sindarin material, illustrated by *athab* (*PE22:167*). Strack tentatively derives this from an older *-p(e)*, possibly originating in *-kwe*, but the exact etymology remains uncertain.

This reconstruction does not require establishing that full primitive derivation. It relies instead on Tolkien's attested late Sindarin suffix, attached to the independently developed copular stem: 

    ✶nā → S naw
  
    naw + -b → naub

As in *nauf*, *aw* is written *au* when followed by a consonant.

**Status:** Hypothetical late Neo-Sindarin morphological formation based on the attested 1969 inclusive verbal suffix. The primitive origin of *-b* remains uncertain.

Source: Eldamo: Subject suffixes, *PE22:167*.

Second-person singular familiar: *naug* — "thou art"

The primitive familiar second-person pronoun ✶*ki* is attested in *VT49:50, 52*. The Sindarin familiar second-person verbal ending *-g* is documented in Tolkien's 1962 paradigm (*PE17:132*), including the form *galog*. We reconstruct an early attachment of the primitive pronoun: 

    ✶nā + ✶ki → ✶nāki
    ✶nāki → nāke → nāk → nǭk → nauk → naug

The changes are:

  1. Final *-i → -e*.
  2. Final short *-e* disappears.
  3. Long *ā → ǭ*.
  4. *ǭ → au*.
  5. Postvocalic *k → g*.

An alternative primitive formation with the pronoun already reduced to *-k* yields the same result: 

    ✶nāk → nǭk → nauk → naug

**Status:** Plausibly derived Neo-Sindarin reconstruction. Both the primitive familiar pronoun and the later Sindarin ending are attested, although the complete copular conjugation is not.

Sources:

  • Eldamo: ✶ki, *VT49:50, 52*
  • Eldamo: Sindarin subject suffixes, *PE17:132*
Second-person singular polite: *naul* — "you are"

The primitive polite second-person pronoun ✶*le* occurs in *VT49:50, 52*. Tolkien's 1969 Sindarin verbal material employs the polite singular suffix *-l*, probably influenced by Quenya according to Strack. Two reconstructions are possible, and both produce the same surface form: 

    **Early attachment:**
    ✶nāle → nāl → nǭl → naul
  
    **Late attachment:**
    ✶nā → S naw
    naw + -l → naul

The early attachment involves loss of final *-e*, followed by *ā → ǭ → au*. Given Strack's suggestion of Quenya influence on the later Sindarin polite pronoun, late attachment may offer the more plausible morphological history.

**Status:** Plausibly derived Neo-Sindarin reconstruction, supported by an attested primitive pronoun and a later Sindarin verbal suffix.

Source: Eldamo: ✶le, *VT49:50–52*; *PE22:167*.

Second-person singular archaic: *naudh* — "you are"

The earlier Sindarin polite pronoun †*de*, "thee/you", occurs in *PE17:26*; Tolkien subsequently replaced it with *le* in the relevant notes. The 1962 Sindarin paradigm uses polite verbal *-ð*, written *-dh*. Strack suggests this might descend from an earlier *-d(e)* and tentatively associates it with Doriathrin usage. Assuming an earlier attachable form *-de*, we reconstruct: 

    ✶nāde → nāðe → nāð → nǭð → nauð → naudh

The principal developments are:

  1. Postvocalic *d → ð*.
  2. Loss of final short *-e*.
  3. Long *ā → ǭ*.
  4. *ǭ → au*.

In ordinary Sindarin spelling, *nauð* is written *naudh*. For this paradigm, *naudh* is retained as an archaic or dialectal alternative to *naul*.

**Status:** Plausibly derived Neo-Sindarin reconstruction, with a hypothetical early attachment. Its identification as specifically Doriathrin is tentative.

Sources:

  • Eldamo: †de, *PE17:26*
  • Eldamo: Subject suffixes, *PE17:132*
Second-person plural familiar: *naugir* — "you are"

The 1962 Sindarin paradigm provides the second-person plural familiar suffix *-gir*, corresponding to singular *-g*. Strack observes that second-person plural endings commonly consist of the corresponding singular ending plus *-ir*. The proposed plural formation is therefore: 

    naug + -ir → naugir

This is treated as suffixation within the developed Sindarin verbal system, not as an inherited Primitive Eldarin conjugation.

**Status:** Hypothetical Neo-Sindarin morphological formation supported by the attested Sindarin ending *-gir*.

Source: Eldamo: Subject suffixes, *PE17:132*.

Second-person plural polite: *naulir* — "you are"

The proposed singular polite form is *naul*, using Tolkien's later Sindarin suffix *-l*. Following the general second-person plural pattern, the plural is formed analogically: 

    naul + -ir → naulir

Unlike familiar *-gir* and archaic polite *-dhir*, a late Sindarin plural polite suffix *-lir* is not established by Tolkien's surviving 1969 material. The form is therefore an extrapolation from the productive second-person plural pattern rather than a directly supported conjugation.

**Status:** Analogical Neo-Sindarin reconstruction; less securely supported than *naugir* or *naudhir*.

Source: Eldamo: Subject suffixes, comparing *PE17:132* and *PE22:167*.

Second-person plural archaic: *naudhir* — "you are"

Tolkien's 1962 paradigm provides the second-person plural polite suffix *-ðir*, corresponding to singular *-ð*. Applying this established ending to the proposed archaic singular form: 

    naudh + -ir → naudhir

This preserves the same relationship between singular and plural found in Tolkien's earlier Sindarin pronominal system.

**Status:** Hypothetical Neo-Sindarin morphological formation using an attested 1962 suffix. Its specifically Doriathrin or archaic interpretation remains tentative.

Source: Eldamo: Subject suffixes, *PE17:132*.

Third-person plural: *nar* — "they are"

Sindarin uses the plural agreement suffix *-r* for the third-person plural. Unlike the first and second persons, this ending is not necessarily pronominal in origin: it marks plural agreement and can also express "they" when no independent subject is present.

The ordinary inflection of Sindarin basic verbs demonstrates that plural forms need not derive directly from their uninflected singular surface forms, for example *câr* → *cerir*. However, *nā-* is unusual because its primitive stem ends in a long vowel rather than a consonant.

Helge Fauskanger discusses vowel-final verbal stems whose third-person plural is formed by adding *-r* after the stem vowel has shortened. Strack also considers the possibility that plural *-r* was added comparatively late. Adopting this approach, we propose a shortened inflectional stem: 

    ✶nā- ⇝ *na-
    *na- + -r → nar

This form is preferred over the alternative *naur*, which would arise from adding *-r* directly to the fully developed *naw*. The crucial assumption is that the copula acquired a shortened plural inflectional stem, by analogy with vowel-final verbs. Such a stem is not independently attested.

**Status:** Analogical Neo-Sindarin reconstruction, not an established phonological outcome of primitive *nā-r*.

Sources:

  • Eldamo: Sindarin verb inflections, especially third-person plurality
  • Ardalambion: Reconstructing the Sindarin Verb System

These forms constitute a **provisional Neo-Sindarin paradigm**. They are not presented as an attested or definitive reconstruction of Tolkien's Sindarin. The system deliberately combines inherited phonological developments with later morphological suffixation and analogy. This is compatible with a productive irregular copula, provided each formation is identified accurately and the resulting paradigm is tested for phonological, morphological and syntactic consistency.

Past tense

Historical foundations

The past tense of the copular verb *na-* is reconstructed from Common Eldarin *nē*, an independent past-tense element meaning "was". Tolkien discusses this element in *VT49/30–31* and *PE22/96*, explaining that it could function as a past-tense base as well as a temporal expression meaning "ago".

It underwent the regular Sindarin development of long *ē* into *ī*: 

CE. nē → S. nî

Paul Strack independently proposed Neo-Sindarin *nî* ("was") and *nîr* ("were") in 2018.

The following paradigm extends *nî* through the established Sindarin pronominal suffixes, drawing primarily upon Tolkien's paradigms in *PE17/132* and *PE22/167*. The inflections are formed within the Sindarin verbal system rather than being treated as inherited Common Eldarin subject-inflected forms.

All the complete conjugations remain **Neo-Sindarin reconstructions**, not attested Tolkienian forms.

First-person singular: *nîn* — "I was" 

nî + -n → nîn

The first-person singular suffix *-n* is consistently documented throughout Tolkien's later Sindarin verbal paradigms (*PE17/132; PE22/167*).

The form can also be supported phonologically by a hypothetical earlier combination with primitive *ni* ("I"): 

nēni → nēne → nīne → nīn

The proposed result is *nîn*, following Sindarin orthography for a long vowel in a stressed monosyllable.

First-person plural exclusive: *nîf* — "we were, excluding the listener" 

nî + -f → nîf

The first-person plural exclusive suffix *-f* /v/ occurs in Tolkien's 1969 Sindarin material (*PE22/167*), exemplified by *athof*.

Strack suggests that this ending derives from primitive *-m(e)*, associated with the exclusive pronoun *me*.

A hypothetical inherited formation would also yield the proposed form: 

nēme → nēm → nīm → nīv

The resulting final /v/ is written *f* in Sindarin.

Thus, *nîf* is supported by both the established later suffix and a plausible historical phonological development.

First-person plural inclusive: *nîb* — "we were, including the listener" 

nî + -b → nîb

The suffix *-b* is documented in Tolkien's 1969 Sindarin material (*PE22/167*), notably in *athab* ("we will", inclusive).

Its precise primitive origin remains uncertain. Strack tentatively derives it from an earlier *-p(e)*, potentially connected with *-kwe*.

The reconstruction therefore uses the established Sindarin suffix directly, without requiring a hypothetical Primitive Eldarin conjugation.

Second-person singular familiar: *nîg* — "thou wast" 

nî + -g → nîg

The familiar second-person singular suffix *-g* is documented in *PE17/132*, including Tolkien's conjugated form *galog*.

Its probable origin is the primitive second-person pronoun *ki* ("thou"), recorded in *VT49/50, 52*. The development from *k* to *g* is consistent with Sindarin postvocalic voicing.

A hypothetical earlier formation *nēki* provides a corresponding phonological explanation: 

nēki → nēke → nīke → nīk → nīg

Second-person singular polite: *nîl* — "you were" 

nî + -l → nîl

The polite second-person singular suffix *-l* occurs in Tolkien's 1969 Sindarin paradigm (*PE22/167*).

Strack connects it with the polite pronoun *le*, probably adopted into Sindarin usage through Quenya influence.

An earlier hypothetical combination *nēle* could yield the same result: 

nēle → nēl → nīl

However, late suffixation is preferred as the morphological explanation, given the proposed borrowing history of *-l*.

Second-person singular archaic: *nîdh* — "you were" 

nî + -dh → nîdh

Tolkien's 1962 Sindarin paradigm (*PE17/132*) uses the polite second-person suffix *-ð*, written *-dh*.

Strack suggests that this form derives from an older *-d(e)*, associated with the pronoun *de* ("thee/you") in *PE17/26*. He tentatively identifies the ending with older or Doriathrin usage.

A hypothetical historical formation would develop as follows: 

nēde → nēðe → nīðe → nīð

The final form is written *nîdh*.

For this paradigm, it is retained as an archaic or dialectal alternative to *nîl*, without asserting that Tolkien attested the copular form in Doriathrin.

Second-person plural familiar: *nígir* — "you were" 

nî + -gir → nígir

The familiar second-person plural suffix *-gir* is documented in *PE17/132*. It corresponds to singular *-g*, extended by the plural element *-ir*.

Accordingly, the form may also be analysed as: 

nîg + -ir → nígir

The long vowel is written with an acute accent in the resulting polysyllabic form, assuming its quantity is preserved.

Second-person plural polite: *nílir* — "you were" 

nî + -lir → nílir

This form extends the later polite singular suffix *-l* through the second-person plural element *-ir*: 

nîl + -ir → nílir

Although the singular suffix *-l* is supported by *PE22/167*, the complete polite plural suffix *-lir* is not directly established in Tolkien's surviving Sindarin paradigm.

The form is therefore an **analogical Neo-Sindarin reconstruction**, designed to preserve the singular–plural distinction within the later polite system.

Second-person plural archaic: *nídhir* — "you were" 

nî + -dhir → nídhir

Tolkien's 1962 paradigm (*PE17/132*) documents the polite second-person plural suffix *-ðir*, corresponding to singular *-ð*.

It can be analysed as: 

nîdh + -ir → nídhir

This form is retained as the plural counterpart of archaic or dialectal *nîdh*.

The historical suffix is attested, although the complete copular conjugation remains reconstructed.

Third-person singular: *nî* — "he/she/it was" 

CE. nē → S. nî

The third-person singular is represented by the uninflected past stem, consistent with Sindarin verbal morphology.

The primitive element *nē* was explicitly described by Tolkien as an independent past form meaning "was" (*VT49/30–31; PE22/96*).

Its long *ē* regularly developed into Sindarin *ī*, yielding the proposed monosyllabic *nî*.

Unlike the personal conjugations above, this form requires no additional pronominal suffix.

Third-person plural: *nîr* — "they were" 

nî + -r → nîr

The suffix *-r* is the regular Sindarin third-person plural agreement marker, documented in *PE17/132*.

Its origins are supported by *PE22/93–94*, where Tolkien identifies *-r/-l* as Common Eldarin plural subject markers.

The suffix indicates plural agreement rather than deriving from an independent pronoun meaning "they".

Paul Strack independently proposes *nîr* as the Neo-Sindarin plural counterpart of *nî*.

The paradigm assumes that the inherited long vowel remains long when the personal suffixes are added. Its spelling follows the Sindarin convention of using circumflexes in stressed monosyllables and acute accents in polysyllables.

While *nî* and *nîr* have independent precedent in Strack's Neo-Sindarin proposals, the complete personal paradigm is a further reconstruction. In particular, *nílir* involves analogical extension of the later polite suffix, and the use of *nîdh* and *nídhir* as archaic alternatives remains tentative.

Future tense

The future tense below is currently being reviewed in light of the present and past tenses reconstructions above, so it will change.
  • **Derivation:**
    • **Stem:** basic verbs form the future with the suffix *-tha* (*-atha* after consonant-final stems): √NA + *-tha* → **natha-**.
    • **Vowel change:** before pronominal endings, the final *-a-* of the stem becomes *-o-* (*nathon* "I will be"), while it is regularly kept as *-a* in the uninflected 3rd person singular (*natha*).
  • **Attested forms:** in his late draft notes for the *Túrin Wrapper* (*VT50:5*), Tolkien wrote the future copula as **natho** or **tho** "will be", and **nathon** "I will be" is attested directly (*VT50:5*). The attested 3rd person singular is therefore *natho* rather than the regular *natha*.

Imperative and non-finite forms

  • **Imperative no** "be!": from Primitive Eldarin *✶nā* → Old Sindarin *✶nō* → **no**. Besides commands, the imperative is also used to express wishes, possibilities, and hypothetical conditions ("may it be / let it be"):
    • **no aer i eneth lín** = "hallowed be thy name" (Sindarin Lord's Prayer, *Ae Adar Nín*, *VT44:21*)
    • Plural ("be ye!"): **nor** / **nogir**, if plural or pronominal suffixes are added to the imperative.
  • **Infinitive nei** (or **nae**) "to be": basic verbs add *✶-i*, with i-affection on the stem vowel (*✶na-i* → *nei*).
  • **Gerund nad** (or **naed**) "being": the common Sindarin word *nad* "thing" literally originated as this gerund of √NA ("that which is").
  • **Present participle naol** (or **neiel**) "being": from the suffix *-ol*, or historical *-iel* (*✶na-iel* → *neiel*).
  • **Past participle nanen** (or **nen**) "been": from the passive/perfect participle ending *-nen* attached to the root.
